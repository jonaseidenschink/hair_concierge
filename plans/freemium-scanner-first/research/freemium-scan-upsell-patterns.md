# Freemium "Scan → Verdict → Upsell" Patterns — Market Research

Date: 2026-09-05. Web research only, read-only, no strategy recommendation (per task scope). Facts are cited; commentary/inference is flagged as such. Pricing and feature splits change frequently — treat anything not from an official help/pricing page as possibly stale.

---

## 1. Yuka (food + cosmetic scanner, France, ~73M users)

**Confirmed free tier:**
- Barcode scanning is unlimited and fully free — "no cap on scanning attempts or reports." [Yuka Help](https://help.yuka.io/l/en/article/hkzw2hkj5w-cost-membership)
- Free tier includes the scan, the health grade, and the ingredient breakdown. [Barefoot Basil review](https://barefootbasil.com/yuka-app-review/)
- "Better alternatives" recommendations for poorly-graded products are part of the free experience — "For any product with a negative grade, Yuka will recommend in total independence a similar product that's better for your health." [Yuka alternatives coverage](https://osana.co/blog/yuka-alternatives) — **note: this is inferred from secondary sources, not a Yuka official page; not independently confirmed as free vs. gated.**

**Confirmed premium ("Yuka Premium") features**, per Yuka's own help center: [Yuka Help — paid version features](https://help.yuka.io/l/en/article/dop80j54bb-paid-version-features)
1. **Advanced search bar** — look up a product's grade by typing name/brand/category, without scanning a barcode.
2. **Offline mode** — scan and see grades without a network connection (relevant in supermarkets/basements with poor signal).
3. **Personalized dietary alerts** — flags for vegetarian/vegan, palm-oil-free, gluten-free, lactose-free, pork-free, soy-free, sulfite-free.

Other sources also mention **longer scan history** as part of Premium, but this wasn't in Yuka's own help article — flagged as **unconfirmed/secondary**. [nutrasafe.co.uk](https://nutrasafe.co.uk/nutrasafe-vs-yuka)

**Pricing:** Annual subscription, pay-what-you-want style sliding scale — €10 / €15 / €20 per year (equivalents: $10/£10/€10 baseline, up to $20 AUD). [Yuka Premium page](https://yuka.io/en/premium-member/), [Yuka Help — why different tariffs](https://help.yuka.io/l/en/article/b5wkrfepnp-why-are-there-different-tariffs)

**Why they keep scanning free (Yuka's own stated rationale):** Yuka states it is "100% financed by user fees with no sponsored content, no affiliate revenue, and no brands buying better scores," and frames premium/book sales as what lets it stay independent of the food/cosmetics industry it grades. [US Chamber of Commerce founder interview](https://www.uschamber.com/co/good-company/the-leap/yuka-app-organic-growth) The commercial logic: the core trust-building product (the grade) must be free and appear un-monetizable-by-brands for the grade to be credible; revenue instead comes from *convenience* features (search without scanning, offline, personalization) layered on top of an already-trusted free core.

**Revenue (secondary/estimate sources, not Yuka-official):**
- 2020 revenue breakdown reported as: €777,771 from premium app sales, €485,826 from a "Healthy Eating Guide" book, €271,826 from a seasonal calendar. [FoodTimes](https://www.foodtimes.eu/consumers-and-health/yuka-app-nutrition-health-and-market-opportunities/)
- 2023 revenue reported at ~$20.3M with 79 employees. Same source also cites a 2024 ARR figure of $7.4M from a different estimator (getlatka) — **the two figures are inconsistent with each other, both are third-party estimates, treat as directional only, not confirmed.** [FoodTimes](https://www.foodtimes.eu/consumers-and-health/yuka-app-nutrition-health-and-market-opportunities/), [Getlatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/yuka.io)
- No conversion-rate (free→paid %) figure was found in public sources.

## 2. Vivino (wine scanner + marketplace)

**Confirmed free tier:**
- Full scan-and-discover flow: scan a wine label, see a basic version of the personalized "Match for You" score, browse the marketplace. [Vivino — complete guide](https://www.vivino.com/en/wine-news/the-complete-guide-to-the-vivino-experience)
- Can add wines to a personal "cellar," but capped — one user-reported cap on the German Play Store listing is **20 bottles** before Premium is required (secondary/anecdotal, not from Vivino's own pricing page). [Grape Guru](https://www.grapeguru.de/en/knowledge/articles/beste-wein-app)

**Confirmed premium features:**
- The **exact numeric score** for every wine (vs. a coarser free version). [Vivino Premium](https://www.vivino.com/en/premium)
- Full cellar tracking: unlimited bottles, desktop access, estimated collection value, drinking-window indicators. [Vivino — complete guide](https://www.vivino.com/en/wine-news/the-complete-guide-to-the-vivino-experience)
- Deeper per-bottle insight ("Wine Adventures" style/region/taste-profile fit) and free shipping thresholds on marketplace orders. [Vivino Premium update page](https://www.vivino.com/en/articles/us-24-premium-update-dual)

**Pricing:** ~$4.99/€4.99 per month, or ~$47.90/€47.90 per year, in the US and most European markets. [Vivino Pricing Guide](https://www.vivino.com/en/articles/premium-pricing-guide-en)

**Upsell/backlash mechanics (commentary, not Vivino-official):**
- Multiple user reports describe Vivino **introducing ads for free users and moving previously-free functionality (e.g., unlimited label scanning) behind the paywall over time**, generating notable backlash from long-time contributors who had built up years of ratings under an app that used to feel fully free. [FileFlap — Subscription Fatigue](https://fileflap.net/blog/subscription-fatigue-the-revolt-against-paywalls/), user-review aggregation at [AnecdoteAI](https://www.anecdoteai.com/insights/vivino)
- This is presented in commentary as a **cautionary example of "closing the barn door after users already invested data/history in the free product"** — i.e., retroactively gating something users had come to rely on free, as opposed to Yuka/CodeCheck's approach of stating scan limits/free-tier scope up front.

## 3. Calorie/food trackers: MyFitnessPal, Yazio, Lifesum

### MyFitnessPal — the barcode-scanner paywall case study
- **What happened:** Effective **October 1, 2022**, MyFitnessPal moved barcode scanning — a feature free for roughly a decade — behind its Premium tier ($19.99/month or $79.99/year at the time). [Pocket-lint](https://www.pocket-lint.com/apps/news/162386-wow-myfitnesspal-put-its-popular-barcode-scanner-feature-behind-a-paywall/), [Droid-Life](https://www.droid-life.com/2022/08/24/myfitnesspal-puts-barcode-scanner-behind-premium-paywall/)
- **User backlash:** Widely covered as one of the more resented app monetization moves of 2022 — described in commentary as "a constant string of 'fuck you' to consumers"; critics noted the free alternative (manual name search) is materially worse because of duplicate/incorrect entries in the food database. [Slashdot thread](https://news.slashdot.org/story/22/08/25/1955238/myfitnesspal-paywalls-barcode-scanner-that-made-counting-calories-easy), [punishedbacklog.com](https://punishedbacklog.com/hey-myfitnesspal-were-not-paying-for-a-damn-barcode-scanner/)
- **Business outcome:** No official MyFitnessPal or parent-company (Francisco Partners, which acquired MFP from Under Armour in 2020) financial disclosure was found confirming subscriber lift or revenue impact from this specific change — **this remains unconfirmed**, despite extensive searching.
- **Pattern continued/escalated:** Secondary sources (2026-dated blog coverage, not verified against an official MFP announcement) describe MyFitnessPal **further tightening the free tier** — capping a "Scan-a-Meal" photo-logging feature at 5/day for free users then to 0, and gating recipe import / macro-by-meal — alongside a reported **rating drop from 4.6 to 4.2 stars on iOS within 48 hours** of a May-2026 change (attributed to Sensor Tower data via a blog, not directly verified against App Store data). [Consumer Tech Wire](https://consumertechwire.com/news/myfitnesspal-paywall-expansion-may-2026/), [Unstar](https://unstar.app/blog/is-myfitnesspal-premium-worth-it-paywall-app-reviews-2026) — **flag: these 2026 figures come from SEO/review-farm-style blogs rather than primary sources; treat as unverified.**

### Yazio
- Free tier **retains barcode scanning** as a differentiator versus MyFitnessPal — repeatedly cited as Yazio's competitive angle against MFP's paywalled scanner. [Intake Nutrition comparison](https://www.intakenutrition.io/blog/yazio-vs-myfitnesspal-comparison-which-calorie-tracker-fits-your-goals-in-2026)
- Free: calorie tracking, barcode scan, basic fasting timer, macro/nutrient logging via database search or scan.
- PRO (~$47.90/yr, one source cites $39.99/yr — **pricing conflicts across sources, treat as approximate**): meal plans, recipes, extended statistics, ad removal, advanced fasting protocols, AI-assisted logging. [NutriScan](https://nutriscan.app/blog/posts/yazio-pricing-2026-free-vs-pro-what-pro-unlocks-33b26f8fc7)

### Lifesum
- Free tier is described across multiple review sites as **the most restricted of the major trackers**: barcode scanning nominally available, but sources conflict — at least one describes the practical free experience as "you can search for food but cannot scan a barcode," while another lists scanning as part of the free tier. **This is a direct contradiction in the secondary sources; not resolved by primary Lifesum pricing pages in this pass.** [Nutrola](https://nutrola.app/en/blog/lifesum-free-vs-premium-what-do-you-actually-get)
- Premium (~$30.99–$99.99/yr depending on term) unlocks named diet plans (keto, Mediterranean, fasting), macro breakdowns, recipes, and detailed feedback.
- Multiple reviewers describe the free tier as designed to feel like "a guided demo" with frequent upgrade prompts, intended to push conversion within the first week. **This is reviewer commentary/opinion, not a Lifesum-stated design goal.**

## 4. General freemium scanner-app patterns

**Pattern A — Scan itself stays free, "search without scanning" is the upsell (Yuka, arguably the cleanest example).** The verdict/grade is free; the convenience of not needing the physical product in hand is paid. This preserves the "always free to check what's in your hand" trust promise while monetizing a genuinely separate use case (planning/pre-shopping research).

**Pattern B — Metered scans, paid tier removes the meter (CodeCheck, MyFitnessPal's now-paywalled scanner, some skincare scanners).**
- **CodeCheck** (Germany, direct food+cosmetics scanner competitor, arguably the closest structural analog to a German market app): free tier = **5 scans per week**, refilling after **6 days** once exhausted; CodeCheck Plus (**€1.99/mo or €11.99/yr**) removes the ads, unlocks unlimited scans, full ingredient info, custom favorite-lists, and bookmarked guide content. [CodeCheck via iOSapps.de](https://iosapps.de/apps/codecheck/), [Verbraucherzentrale review](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.de/wissen/umwelt-haushalt/produkte/apptest-codecheck-ein-barcodescan-zeigt-was-drin-ist-93315)
- MyFitnessPal shows the harder version of this pattern — going from unlimited free scanning to **zero** free scans — and drew the most public backlash of any example found in this research, because it retroactively removed a feature users already depended on daily, rather than launching with the meter from day one.

**Pattern C — Verdict/grade free, deep detail or personalization paid (Yuka, INCI Beauty, ThinkDirty, EWG Healthy Living — the dominant pattern among ingredient-safety scanners).**
- **INCI Beauty**: free = unlimited scan, unlimited search, full product-detail/composition view, multi-product analysis, community, history/favorites, basic routine-building. Premium (**$14.99**, one-time IAP per source found) = no ads, analysis of products **without a barcode**, ingredient-based search filters, offline mode. [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/inci-beauty/id1276113963), [INCI Beauty features page](https://incibeauty.com/en/features)
- **Think Dirty**: "Searching and viewing safety scores are always free." [Think Dirty FAQ](https://www.thinkdirtyapp.com/faqs/) Premium (from **€2.19/week or €4.39/month**, plus a lifetime one-time option) = personalized ingredient-preference alerts, search-by-ingredient deep dives, curated category lists (e.g. "Mineral Sunscreens"), advanced list/tracking features.
- **EWG Healthy Living**: "the core features will always be free" — barcode scan or name search → a simple 1–10 safety score across cosmetics, food, and household cleaners. [EWG Healthy Living](https://healthyliving.ewg.org/) Premium = side-by-side product comparison and persistent ingredient-preference filters reflected as on-product badges. **No price was found in this pass** — EWG's public materials describe the tier but not a specific number.

Across pattern C, the consistent shape is: **the yes/no verdict is always free (that's the trust-building hook); personalization (my preferences/allergens applied automatically) and comparison/planning tools are what's paid.**

**Pattern D — "Alternatives/recommendations" as an upsell lever.** Weak evidence either way in this research pass. Yuka's "better alternatives for a badly-graded product" appears to be **free**, based on secondary/aggregator descriptions (not confirmed on Yuka's own help pages) — suggesting that even the more monetizable-sounding "here's what to buy instead" feature isn't necessarily gated in the leading example. **This one data point should not be over-weighted; it comes from third-party "Yuka alternatives" round-up articles, not Yuka itself, and no example was found of a competitor explicitly monetizing "alternatives" as a standalone premium line item.**

**Pattern E — History/saved lists as upsell.** Present in **CodeCheck** (favorites/custom lists explicitly named as a Plus-only feature) and mentioned as likely-premium in secondary Yuka commentary ("longer scan history"), but not confirmed on Yuka's own help page. General industry commentary (not scanner-specific) treats saved-lists/favorites as a "genuine user need" trigger point for paywalls generally, alongside storage limits and collaboration features — but no scanner-specific data was found quantifying its conversion power versus other levers. [Eleken paywall examples](https://www.eleken.co/blog-posts/paywall-examples)

**Cross-cutting data on which mechanic "converts better" (industry-wide, not scanner-specific):**
- RevenueCat's 2026 subscription-app benchmarks (115,000+ apps, $16B+ tracked revenue) report a **hard paywall median Day-35 trial-to-paid conversion of 10.7%** vs. **2.1% for freemium** — roughly a 5x gap in raw conversion. [RevenueCat — Hard paywall vs. freemium](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/hard-paywall-vs-freemium)
- However, the same analysis notes this **inverts on long-run outcomes in some cases**: one case study saw a **75% LTV increase** switching from hard paywall to a multistep freemium flow, while a different app saw the *same* strategy cause a **50%+ drop in subscriber conversion** — i.e., **outcome is highly product-specific, not a universal freemium-vs-hardwall verdict.**
- RevenueCat also states that **retention converges over a year** regardless of whether the app started as hard-paywall or freemium, and that soft/metered/feature-gated paywalls "let users build habits before paying," trading lower immediate conversion for better long-term retention — but **no source found gives separate, apples-to-apples numbers for "metered scan count" vs. "feature-gated" soft paywalls specifically**; RevenueCat's public writeups group both under "soft paywall."
- 55% of 3-day trial cancellations happen on Day 0 — underscoring that first-session framing (what's shown as free vs. locked, immediately) matters more than the specific gating mechanic long-term. [RevenueCat — State of Subscription Apps 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)

## Summary of confidence levels

- **High confidence (official source, cross-checked):** Yuka's premium feature list and pricing; CodeCheck's scan cap and pricing; Think Dirty's "always free" scoring statement; EWG's "core features always free" statement; MyFitnessPal's Oct-2022 scanner paywall and its price at the time; RevenueCat's headline 10.7%/2.1% conversion benchmark.
- **Medium confidence (consistent across multiple secondary sources, no official primary confirmation):** Vivino's free cellar cap and premium feature set; Yazio's/Lifesum's free-vs-pro splits (some internal contradictions between review sites); Yuka's "alternatives" feature being free.
- **Low confidence / explicitly flagged as unverified:** Yuka's precise revenue/ARR figures (two conflicting third-party estimates); MyFitnessPal's 2026 "further paywall expansion" and star-rating-drop claims (SEO-blog sourced, not verified against App Store data or an MFP statement); any quantified "which upsell lever converts best" claim specific to scanner apps (no such study was found — only general subscription-app benchmarks, which are pattern-agnostic).
